# Discussion of Findings

This study discussed the findings based on the objectives of the project, the Statement of the Problem, and the related literature and systems presented in Chapter II. The results show that the developed system was able to replace the manual paper-based blotter process of Barangay 179 with a centralized platform for recording incidents, generating visual reports and crime statistics, classifying operational risk, displaying heatmap-based locations, and supporting prioritized patrol planning.

The system's existing CART-named operational engine is a transparent, weighted rule-based risk-scoring engine. It uses configured factors such as incident type, time of day, day type, location history, and recent frequency to assign risk scores and risk levels. Because this engine is not trained from labelled observations and does not perform decision-tree fitting, it does not have a statistically valid classification accuracy, confusion matrix, precision, recall, or F1-score. Therefore, the previously reported 88.4% CART accuracy should not be presented as the accuracy of the current operational engine.

The project also includes a separate CART Decision Tree Classifier prototype for weekly street-level occurrence forecasting. This prototype predicts whether at least one qualifying reported incident will occur on a registered street during the following seven-day period. It was evaluated using a chronological synthetic dataset with separate training, validation, and held-out test periods. On the held-out synthetic test period, the prototype obtained an accuracy of 58.85%, precision of 23.23%, recall of 66.85%, and F1-score of 34.48%. The confusion matrix contained 246 true positives, 1,091 true negatives, 813 false positives, and 122 false negatives. These results indicate that the prototype was able to identify a portion of positive street-weeks, but it produced a substantial number of false-positive predictions. Its F1-score did not meet the project's research target of 80%.

The synthetic result must be interpreted as a pipeline-development result rather than evidence that the system can accurately forecast actual Barangay 179 crime. The prototype is labelled **Synthetic Prototype — Not Validated on Real Barangay Data**. It was trained and evaluated using generated observations because the repository does not establish a sufficiently long and complete history of genuine Barangay 179 street-week records for reliable out-of-time validation. The synthetic predictions are stored separately from official incident and blotter records and must not be counted as actual crimes or used as automatic patrol assignments.

Based on the ISO/IEC 25010 evaluation, the three evaluators rated the system as Highly Acceptable, with an overall mean of 4.59. Compatibility, Reliability, Maintainability, and Flexibility obtained the highest means of 4.67, while Functional Suitability and Performance Efficiency obtained the lowest means of 4.44; both remained within the Acceptable interpretation. No criterion received a rating lower than 4, indicating that the system met the expected software-quality requirements for its intended barangay use.

Based on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the respondents generally had a positive perception of the system. The overall TAM mean was 4.39, interpreted as Agree based on the study's scale. Perceived Usefulness obtained the highest mean of 4.43, and the heatmap feature received the highest individual rating of 4.67. This supports the findings of Queiroz Neto et al. (2025) and Ahat et al. (2026), who emphasized that crime mapping is most valuable when it is connected to patrol planning and hotspot identification.

The results also point to areas for improvement. The statements concerning the understanding of crime patterns and trends, learning to use the system, and understanding prediction results received the lowest TAM ratings. The ISO/IEC 25010 evaluation likewise identified functional correctness, resource utilization, and capacity as comparatively weaker criteria. These findings suggest adding short explanations for the operational risk levels and forecast outputs, providing a user guide or onboarding flow for first-time users, improving the model's precision through additional representative data, and testing the system with larger and more complete datasets.

Finally, the evaluation has limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. Most TAM respondents were students, only one respondent was barangay personnel, and two-thirds were first-time users. The system operates offline on a local server, so data cannot be accessed remotely. The reliability of both operational risk scoring and future forecasting depends on the completeness, consistency, and timeliness of records entered by barangay staff. In addition, the reported forecasting metrics are based on synthetic data and cannot be generalized to real Barangay 179 operations. Future evaluations should involve more barangay officials and tanods, use a sufficiently mature real historical dataset, and report a separate out-of-time real-data evaluation before any forecasting model is considered for operational promotion.
